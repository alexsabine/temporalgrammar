2. Counting, and the size of the data

The H01 sample is 1.05 mm³ of cortex imaged in voxels of 4 × 4 × 33 nm, and it occupies 1.4 petabytes (Shapson-Coe et al. 2024). Scaling by volume gives the size of a whole human brain at the same resolution. The brain’s volume is taken from its mean mass of 1,508 g (Azevedo et al. 2009) and a density that is a setting of this page.

A whole brain at electron-microscope resolution Bbrain=BH01×VbrainVH01

A zettabyte is a thousand million terabytes. This is an extrapolation by simple proportion, not a plan anyone has costed.

3. A dendrite is a leaky cable

Let V be the voltage across the membrane, measured from rest, at distance x along a branch of diameter d. Current flows along the core, which has resistivity Ri, and leaks out through the membrane, which has resistance Rm and capacitance Cm for each unit of area. Rall (1959) showed that this gives the cable equation, with two constants: a length λ over which a steady voltage falls to 37%, and a time τ over which the membrane charges.

The cable equation and its two constants λ2∂2V∂x2=τ∂V∂t+V,λ=d⁢Rm4⁢Ri,τ=RmCm V(x)=V0e−x/λ(steady state, a very long cable)

The page solves the equation on the whole tree in the picture. The tree is cut into compartments 10 µm long and stepped forward in time by the implicit method of Hines (1984). A real tree is not one long cable, so the computed ratio differs from the simple exponential: the cell body and the other branches drain current.

Each synapse opens a conductance g(t) towards a reversal potential E of 0 mV. The slow part of it passes through NMDA receptors, which are plugged by magnesium ions at rest and unplugged by depolarisation. Jahr & Stevens (1990) measured the unblocked fraction B. Depolarisation opens more receptors, which depolarises the branch further: that loop is the dendritic spike.

Synaptic current and the magnesium block Isyn=n[gAMPA(t)+gNMDA(t)B(V)](E−V),B(V)=11+[Mg]3.57e−0.062V

Poirazi, Brannon & Mel (2003) showed that a detailed model of a pyramidal cell is well summarised by a two-layer network: each thin branch applies its own S-shaped function to its inputs, and the cell body adds the branches up. The curve in the read-out under the stage is that S-shaped function for the branch you chose, computed from the cable. Beniaguev, Segev & London (2021) later found that matching a full model of one cortical neuron, millisecond by millisecond, took an artificial network five to eight layers deep.

5. Release in packets

Del Castillo & Katz (1954) proposed that transmitter is released in whole packets. If n vesicles are ready, each is released with probability p, and each adds an amount q to the response, then the number released follows the binomial distribution. Here each packet also varies a little in size, with a spread of cv times q.

Quantal release mean=npq,variance=npq2(1−p+cv2),P(nothing)=(1−p)n

Once released, the transmitter crosses the cleft by diffusion. A molecule with diffusion coefficient D wanders a distance x in a time of about x²/2D. The cleft is so narrow that this takes less than a microsecond; the delay at a synapse comes from the steps before and after.

Time to diffuse across the cleft t≈x22D

6. Ions and single channels

An ion with charge z is more concentrated on one side of the membrane than the other. Diffusion pushes it one way; the voltage pushes it back. The two balance at the Nernst potential, where R is the gas constant, T the temperature and F Faraday’s constant.

Nernst potential E=RTzFln[ion]out[ion]in

One open channel of conductance γ passes a current i. In a patch of N channels, each open with probability p, the number open is binomial, so the current has a mean and a variance (Sigworth 1980). Hodgkin and Huxley’s n⁴ is read here as four independent gates per channel, each opening at rate αn and closing at βn; the channel conducts when all four are open. The flicker as a fraction of the mean falls as one over the square root of N, which is why a whole cell’s current looks smooth.

One channel, and many i=γ(V−E),⟨I⟩=Npi,σI2=Np(1−p)i2

A current is charge per second, and each potassium ion carries one elementary charge e. Dividing gives the number of ions.

7. What limits each instrument

MRI is limited by signal. Each voxel must contain enough hydrogen nuclei to be heard above the noise, and halving the voxel’s side leaves an eighth of the signal. Light is limited by diffraction. Abbe (1873) showed that a lens of numerical aperture NA cannot separate two points closer than about half a wavelength, which is why a light microscope cannot see a synaptic cleft.

The diffraction limit of a light microscope d=λ2NA

Electrons have a far shorter wavelength, given by de Broglie’s relation with a correction for their speed. Here h is Planck’s constant, m and e the electron’s mass and charge, V the accelerating voltage and c the speed of light. Wavelength is not what holds an electron microscope back. The electrons destroy the specimen, so each molecule can be given only a small dose, and each image is faint. Nakane et al. (2020) averaged 363,126 images of separate, identical molecules to reach 1.22 Å. In a connectome the limit is different again: the size of the voxel, and the patience to cut and image thousands of sections.

The wavelength of an electron λ=h2meV(1+eV2mc2)
