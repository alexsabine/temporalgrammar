Time on this page: a CRR reading

This site is where Coherence, Rupture, Regeneration is worked on. CRR is Alexander Sabine’s proposal for a grammar of change in time. It is an interpretive framework under test. It is not established physics, and none of the scientists cited on this page proposed it or endorses it.

The zoom above is about size. This section is about time, which was in the page all along. It keeps three kinds of statement apart, and each paragraph says which it is:

Physics established results, computed by the page. Imagined where no measurement reaches. CRR reading the site’s own interpretation.

Everything computed here is an exact identity or standard physics put in other words. None of it is evidence for CRR. It is a reading, and the section ends by saying what it is not.

CRR in four lines C counts every step a system has taken since its last cut, steps back included. C* is how far it now is from where it began. The cut comes the first time it reaches the opposite state, half a turn away: C*Ω = 1, with Ω = 1/π. Whatever it walked beyond the direct way is surplus, S = C − C*.

CRR’s three equations, as the programme states them
Coherence: the past accumulates, since the last cut C(t)=1σ∫tntL(τ)dτ,L(τ)=g(θτ)|θ˙τ|
Rupture: the first arrival at the opposite state δ(t−tn+1),tn+1=inf{t>tn:C*Ω=1}
Regeneration: the future is built from weighted memory mn+1=∑j=−1nwjΦj∑j=−1nwj,wj=qn−j·eSjΩ

θ is the system’s state on a circle of probability distributions, g is the Fisher information (how distinguishable neighbouring states are), and L is the Fisher–Rao speed. σ is a resolution, calibrated separately: the smallest change the system can tell apart. So C is accumulated arc length since the last rupture tn, in units of σ: every step taken, including steps back. C* is the geodesic distance from where the occasion began, and Ω = 1/(the manifold’s diameter) in the same units, so the cut comes the first time the system reaches the state opposite to where it began. Any wandering on the way is surplus, S = C − C* ≥ 0.

Regeneration rebuilds from what each completed occasion settled (Φ). Only completed occasions count. Memory is weighted by recency q and by salience, eSΩ: surplus counted in half-turns. Salience is a proposal still to be tested. For a yes-or-no system the diameter is π, so Ω = 1/π. In this section lengths are given in half-turns, that is, already multiplied by Ω, with σ = 1 radian unless the resolution slider says otherwise.

1. Both ends of this page are the beginning

Physics Flying out is looking back in time. Light from the widest frames set out long ago, so the far end of the zoom shows the young universe. The page has said so already.

Physics Diving in is looking back too. To probe a length λ takes an energy E = hc/λ. The universe was once so hot that an ordinary particle in it carried that much energy. So every stop below the atom matches a moment: the last time the whole universe was as hot as that collision. The smaller the length, the earlier the moment.

The Large Hadron Collider’s 13.6 TeV matches 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ s after the beginning. The first hard line of this page, 35.8 TeV, matches 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁶ s. One GeV matches 3.1 × 10⁻⁷ s, and one MeV 0.74 s. These are rough by nature: “as hot as” is taken to mean kT = E, and a different fair convention moves each time about ninefold.

So the depth gauge is really a loop. Now is in the middle, and both unseen ends are the earliest moments. Telescopes cannot see earlier than 370,000 years, when the fog cleared. Accelerators reach the energies of 10⁻¹⁶ s. A collision is not a small Big Bang, though: it reaches that energy in a speck of space for an instant, and not that heat everywhere.

Physics Where exactly is now on the gauge? Not quite at the reader. Today’s sky is at 2.7255 K, and the wavelength that matches that warmth is 5.3 mm, the size of a lentil. Frames wider than that on the way in, the whole brain among them, match a sky colder than today’s. They belong to the future. A sky cold enough to match 1.7 m comes 114 billion years after the beginning.

Imagined At the Planck energy the same formula gives three hundredths of the Planck time. It has no meaning there. Nobody knows what “time after the beginning” means at that end.

The picture of the largest and the smallest meeting is not new. Sheldon Glashow drew the universe as a snake swallowing its tail, which Primack and Abrams (2006) call the cosmic uroboros. There the meeting stood for a hoped-for joining of the theories of the large and the small. Here it is plainer: both ends are early.

Physics, and a coincidence The geometric mean of the Planck length and the horizon’s radius is 84 µm: the width of a hair, or a large cell. The horizon is growing, so this belongs to the present epoch and to nothing deeper.

2. The contents of the universe as one path

Physics The universe holds radiation, matter and dark energy. Their shares change as it expands. Radiation thins fastest, matter next, dark energy not at all. At first almost everything was radiation. In the far future almost everything will be dark energy. The numbers here are Planck 2018, with a constant dark energy.

Geometry Three shares that add up to one can be treated as a probability distribution: pick a joule of the universe at random and ask which kind it is. That is a choice of this page, not a discovery. The standard measure of distance between such distributions is the Fisher–Rao distance, and with it all possible mixtures form one-eighth of a sphere. The three pure states are its corners. Each corner is half a turn, π, from each of the others.

Geometry The universe did not take the direct way. It went from radiation almost all the way to matter, which reached 99.53% of the contents at z = 11.2. Then it turned, and it is now heading for dark energy. The whole path is 1.949 half-turns long, for a displacement of one. The surplus is 0.949. Today 1.569 of it is walked: we are 80.5% of the way along.

By the strict rule the cut never comes. In this picture the opposite of “all radiation” is any state with no radiation in it, and the distance from the start is already 0.994 of a half-turn. It reaches 1 only when the last trace of radiation is gone, which takes for ever.

CRR reading In CRR’s words the whole matter era is surplus: wandering on the way from radiation to its opposite. It is also the only era in which things that remember are made: galaxies, stars, chemistry, readers.

Physics There is physics under that. Gravity gathers matter into structure only while matter is a large share of the contents. The growth rate of structure is close to Ωm(a)0.55 (Linder 2005), where Ωm(a) is matter’s share at the time: growth goes as the path’s nearness to the matter corner. The page integrates the growth equation itself and finds the rule good to 0.003 since z = 5. Lumps of matter have deepened about 1,600-fold since matter overtook radiation. All the future holds is a further factor of 1.41, however long it lasts. Growth freezes.

Physics Aaronson, Carroll and Ouellette (2014) studied a simple model, cream stirring into coffee, and found that its entropy only rises while its complexity rises and then falls. Their paper is about that model. The parallel with this path is drawn here, not by them. The beginning, hot smooth radiation, was simple. The end, empty space with dark energy, will be simple. Everything interesting is on the detour.

A caution. The shape is geometry, not a finding. Any history that passes through three states in turn, each nearly pure, walks about two half-turns to be displaced by one. A made-up universe of three invented fluids gives 1.99. One in which the middle stage never dominates gives 1.33. Only the particular numbers, 1.949 and 99.53%, are our universe’s.

3. A steady drift, and a half-turn that takes for ever

Physics Expansion shifts the odds at a steady rate. Take any two ingredients and the ratio of their shares. Each time the universe grows by a factor e, the logarithm of that ratio moves by the same amount: 1 for matter against radiation, 3 for dark energy against matter. Equal steps of expansion are equal steps of log-odds. That is exact.

Physics Now take the last leg alone: matter giving way to dark energy. Two shares make a half-circle, and the state is an angle φ on it, from 0 (all matter) to a half-turn (all dark energy). The angle moves slowly at first, fastest exactly half-way, and slowly again. Half-way was at z = 0.295, about a quarter of an e-fold ago. We live just after the moment of fastest change.

In ordinary time the same leg has an exact formula. The fraction of the half-turn covered is (2/π) gd(t/tΛ). Here tΛ = 11.70 billion years is a time set by dark energy alone, and gd is the Gudermannian, the function that turns distance up a Mercator map into latitude. The expansion stopped slowing at 0.392 of the half-turn, 7.70 billion years in. Matter and dark energy were equal at exactly ½, at 10.31 billion years. Now it is 0.620. The remaining 0.380 takes for ever. Like the pole on a Mercator map, the end is at infinity.

This follows in three lines from the textbook solution for such a universe (Ryden 2016), and the fold-out gives them. It is elementary and may well have been noted before. A brief search found the same fact written with a hyperbolic tangent (Chavanis 2015), and the Gudermannian used in a different model of dark energy (Lazkoz et al. 2023), but not this statement.

Physics So how far through are we? It depends on the clock.

The resolution slider

CRR reading This is the key point for CRR. Its cut needs arrival at the opposite state. In smooth cosmology nothing ever arrives: it only comes closer. Unless the observer cannot tell the difference. CRR has a term for that, σ, the smallest change the system can tell apart. Set it here.

With σ = 0.1 the cut comes at 43 billion years. With 0.01, at 70. With a millionth, at 178. No sharpness brings it to “never”, and none makes it certain: it is set by the observer.

The same slider decides whether the clearing of the fog “was” a cut. Hydrogen stopped at 0.990 of its half-turn, because a little stayed ionised (the earlier check 2, below). So recombination counts as a completed occasion only for an observer with σ of 0.031 radians or coarser. That figure depends on how much hydrogen stayed ionised, which this page only sets roughly: the panel shows it for three settings.

4. Hydrogen went there and back

Physics Hydrogen began ionised: bare nuclei and loose electrons. At 370,000 years it became atoms and the fog cleared. Then the first stars and galaxies tore the atoms apart again. Planck 2018 puts the mid-point of that at z = 7.67 ± 0.73, about 670 million years in. Between the galaxies hydrogen has been ionised ever since, as it was at the start.

CRR reading As a path this is 0.990 of a half-turn out and 0.990 back: 1.98 half-turns walked, and no displacement at the end. It is nearly pure surplus. Under the site’s salience proposal, eSΩ, an occasion like that would weigh e1.98 ≈ 7.2 times an occasion with no surplus.

Three cautions. Salience is a proposal still to be tested, and this is an illustration, not a test. Only completed occasions count, and by the strict rule this one never completed: it turned back 0.010 short. And with the resolution slider at 0.031 or coarser the same history reads differently: two completed occasions, out and back, each direct, with no surplus at all. The framework does not yet say which reading is right.

5. What this says about “now”

Path and state. CRR reading C* can be read off two snapshots, the start and the present. C cannot: it needs the whole history. Surplus is exactly what a snapshot forgets and a history keeps. Physics has the same distinction: energy is a quantity of state, while heat and work depend on the path taken. On this reading memory is not a store. It is the difference between a path and its chord.

The present is the past, transformed. Physics That sentence, from this site’s account of CRR, is close to a literal statement of physics. The present state is the earlier state carried forward by the laws, and as far as is known nothing is lost on the way in principle. But those laws run equally well backwards. The difference between past and future comes from somewhere else: the very smooth, low-entropy beginning, which philosophers call the Past Hypothesis (Albert 2000; Penrose 1989). That is why there are records of the past and none of the future. CRR reading C is an arc length, so it can only grow. That puts the arrow in by construction; it does not explain it. Its nearest relative in physics is thermodynamic length, which sets a bound on the heat a change must waste (Crooks 2007).

Now is local. Physics Everything this page shows beyond the reader is on the past light cone. No event on the surface called “now” can be seen, except here. That fits two things CRR says: the present has zero temporal extension, and what the present stands on is the past. The tension should be stated plainly. Relativity has no universal present. Cosmology supplies a preferred time only because the universe is nearly uniform: it is the time of observers for whom the microwave sky looks the same in every direction. That is a fact about our universe, not a law. The standing positions are these. The block universe: all events exist, and “now” is like “here”. The growing block: the past exists and the future does not (Broad 1923; Ellis 2006). And Whitehead’s occasions (1929), “the many become one, and are increased by one”, to which CRR is closest.

Cuts need a finite observer. CRR reading Smooth physics gives asymptotes. Matter never quite reached 100%. Hydrogen never became quite neutral. The last half-turn takes for ever: an endless time folded into a finite arc, as in Zeno. A cut happens only relative to a resolution σ. So on this page’s evidence CRR’s cut is not a feature of the cosmos by itself. It is a feature of a finite system reading it, which is what the programme’s fifth commitment says: rupture is a commitment. This is a real constraint on the framework. Applied to cosmology it must declare σ, or accept that cosmic transitions are not ruptures.

The shape of the whole. A simple beginning and a simple end. Between them a detour that is all surplus, where the world’s memory is laid down, and where we are: four-fifths of the way along the path, and near the moment of fastest change.

What this is not

  • It is not evidence for CRR. Every number in this section is an exact identity or standard physics, and would be the same if CRR had never been proposed. Nothing here tests it.
  • It is not the view of the scientists and philosophers cited. They are cited for the physics and for the positions named, not for the reading.
  • The three-corner pattern is generic: two half-turns for one is what three stages in turn give.
  • Treating the shares of the universe’s contents as a probability distribution is a modelling choice. So is using the width of the frame as the length probed.
  • The last leg assumes dark energy is constant. If DESI’s hint that it is fading holds, the leg changes. With DESI’s numbers taken literally and run forward, which they were not made for, dark energy’s share peaks at 84% when the universe is 1.9 times its present size and then falls; the path turns back towards matter, and what looked like the final crossing becomes more surplus.

They are kept here in short, with the numbers still computed by the page. The full wording, the failures on the first run and the bets are in RESULTS.md and BETS.md in the site’s source. Checks 1 and 2 are used again above.

  1. The distance between the two states of a yes-or-no system is π, so Ω = 1/π. Pass Established mathematics, not a finding of CRR.
  2. The clearing of the fog as one CRR occasion. Inconclusive The count is ½ exactly where hydrogen is half ionised, as it must be, and stops short of the cut. The Saha equation in other words.
  3. The stops of this page are spaced with the candidate spread 1/2π. Fail The spread is several times larger, and the stops were the author’s choice.
  4. Matter’s share today is CRR’s Ω = 1/π. Inconclusive True within Planck’s uncertainty, not within DESI’s, and only at about the present. A coincidence of the epoch; the programme does not claim it.
  5. The √π where a mass’s two lengths cross is a sign of CRR. Fail It comes from writing h and not ħ.
  6. Dark energy’s density peaks where w crosses −1 (Cosmic coherence). Pass, as an identity True for any fluid whatever, by the conservation of energy. Not evidence for CRR.
  7. The turning points of cosmic history fall at intervals CRR would expect. Not testable Three events give two intervals.

Two pass, and both are mathematics that does not depend on CRR. Two fail. Three cannot be decided. Nothing in them is evidence for CRR.
