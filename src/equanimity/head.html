Holding Weight Through Time

How much of what you carry to keep, and how much to give to what you meet. Seven scenes on one bench.

What the scenes show

A point. A bell that keeps its weight as it narrows ends as a pin: all the weight in one place. That is the Dirac delta.

A comb, and the count. Points at even spacing make a comb. Add them up as you pass and you get a staircase, one step for each tooth. Adding up is the integral.

Bell and bowl. Take minus the logarithm of the bell and you get a bowl, x²/2s². The bell says how much is there. The bowl says how hard it is to be elsewhere. A narrow bell is a deep well; a wide one is nearly level ground.

Carried and met. The world drifts and is seen through noise. The marble is the estimate. At each meeting it keeps a share of where it was and gives the rest to the glimpse: new = old + K (glimpse − old). The Kalman filter finds the K that makes the error least. When drift and noise are equal that K is 0.618, and the uncertainty breathes between 0.618 and 1.618 for ever.

The cone. A mind that gives K is acting as if its uncertainty before each meeting were K/(1−K). That is the fan of light. Too heavy on the past (K = 0.08): the cone is 0.087 and the real surprise about 6.5, so the cone is far too narrow. Too light (K = 0.97): the cone is 32 and the real surprise about 1.9, so anything might be out there and little is. At balance the cone is the size of the world's real surprises, 1.618.

The path. Too heavy walks about a fifth as far as the world. Too light walks about two thirds further. Balance walks the same distance. The extra distance is not waste when it matches the world's own wandering.

Two who meet. Each takes the other as its glimpse. After one exchange the gap is (1 − K₁ − K₂) times what it was. Gains that add to one meet at once. Both at 0.618 overshoot and swap sides. Both at 0.08 close only 16% of the gap each time. When one gives 0.02 and the other 0.98 they meet where the first one began: the one who does not move decides where the meeting is.

Checked before publishing. The bets on the arithmetic were written down before the model was run. All held but one, and that one failed through a fault in my test, not the model: the grid I named had no point on a tooth. The log is kept with the page's source.

What this leaves out

Almost everything about minds. This is one number drifting on a line, seen through bell-shaped noise, by a filter that already knows how fast the world drifts and how noisy its glimpses are. Real learners have to work those out too (Behrens and colleagues 2007; Nassar and colleagues 2010).

The value 0.618 is not a law. It is the best weight only when drift and noise are equal. If the world is steadier, hold more; if it moves faster, give more. A kept weight of 0.618 is best only when drift is about a quarter of the noise (0.236).

When the dial is set by hand, the bowl and the fan show the uncertainty that would make that weight the right one. That is my choice of what to draw. The error shown is measured.

In the two-marble scene neither is told about the other's noise, and each simply moves a fixed share. Friston and Frith (2015) treat two agents modelling each other properly; this does not.

The bowls are drawn as channels so that the curve shows from the front. Heights are scaled to fit the bench. A bell cannot be drawn narrower than a pin, so the last step to a true point is left to the pin.

Reading too much weight on the past as a picture of anything clinical would go beyond this page. Powers, Mathys and Corlett (2017) is the place to start on that.

Names, and sources

Holding and doing are old ideas with many names. Winnicott wrote of being and doing; an older tradition says yin and yang. Some of those names are gendered. This page uses the plain words.
